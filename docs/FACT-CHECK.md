## Corrections that change a launch decision
- **Slack provider wait.** The guide showed "None" for unlisted public distribution, as if Slack published that figure. It now shows "Not published". The docs only imply no review.
- **Slack rate-limit wording.** The "on or after May 29, 2025" wording covers the updated API Terms, not the rate limits. The rate-limit text says apps "created after May 29, 2025". The claim and its open question were rewritten.
- **GitHub Marketplace.** Paid listings say apps "should" have 200 users, not "need". This is softer than the original claim.
- **GitHub "no warning" claim.** It said users see no unverified-app warning. The live consent screen shows trust signals for new apps: "Not owned or operated by GitHub", "Created less than a day ago", "Fewer than 10 GitHub users".
- **Google 100-user cap.** The guide was unsure whether the cap affects identity-only apps. Console text ties the cap to unapproved sensitive or restricted scopes. The claim is still partially supported, because the project inspected requested no scopes at all.
- **Gmail sensitive-scope timeline.** This is a real conflict between two official Google pages: 10 business days versus 3 to 5. Both sources are now cited and the claim stays "conflicting".

## Patterns
- **Agents were more cautious than wrong.** Several claims marked partial or garbled were fully supported once the raw HTML was read. The fetch tool drops short words and truncates long quotes.
- **Shortened excerpts drop qualifiers.** Examples are "For example," and "only when user consent is enabled". Excerpts are now verbatim.
- **Timing fields attract unsupported values.** Two "None" or "immediate" waits had no published figure behind them. The live generator now enforces "Not published" in code.

### Patterns

- **Cut excerpts lose qualifiers.** Shortening with "..." dropped hedges that change the meaning: "For example," and "only when user consent is enabled" (MS c12), and "should" vs "need" (GH c17). The excerpts were real quotes, but the claim text was stronger than the source.
- **Claims based on missing information** (GH c2, c20; Google c22) were labelled honestly as partially_supported, unverified or inference. Firsthand evidence helped exactly there. It also caught one overstatement: GitHub does show new-app trust signals.
- **Google's cap wording is spread across pages.** The help pages tie the cap to "unverified app screen" or "testing/staging". Only the console text links it explicitly to unapproved sensitive or restricted scopes.
- **Page summaries can differ from the body.** The brand-verification summary says "2-3 business days", while the body says the automated path takes minutes. The guide quotes the body correctly.
- **Big pages need targeted extraction.** The Graph permissions reference (about 578k characters) was cut off by WebFetch before the Mail.Read row, so I checked it from the raw HTML. A link alone can't show the row.

### Patterns

- **The dropped words were a fetch-tool problem, not a source problem.** The raw HTML for every LinkedIn page contains the full sentences. The fetch tool's summary drops short words such as "a", "the", "are" and "to", and so does any large tool output in this session (I saw the same thing with Google pages). Bracketed reconstructions were close but not always right. For example, the pre-fill clause is "provide **a** clear and prominent notice ... edit **such** pre-filled content". Matching the raw HTML with a script is the reliable fix.
- **Status was too low wherever the fetch had been garbled.** Three claims marked partial or "garbled" (Gmail c26, LinkedIn c16 and c24) were fully supported once the raw page was read. LinkedIn c25 was an inference, but an official page covers half of it. In this batch the agents were overly cautious rather than overclaiming.
- **Conflicts need a check of what each sentence is about.** Gmail's 10 vs 3-5 days is a real conflict: same process name on two official pages. Slack's "after" vs "on or after" was not one, because the second phrase governs the Terms of Service date, not the rate limit. Wording that differs is a conflict only if both sentences describe the same thing.
- **Timing values must point at supported claims.** Gmail's sensitive-scope stage used only a `conflicting` claim, so I split it into one supported claim per figure (c30, c31). Slack's providerWait said "None" for unlisted distribution as if it were a published figure. It now says "not published".
